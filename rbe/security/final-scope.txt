Current scope of the joint RBE/anamorphic profile
================================================

The selected profile uses phi=256, q=281474976694273, n=16, B=30,
m_A=32, m_B=16, m_C=64, m_star=126, b=65536, sigma=16,
sigma_tilde=2^36, proof width sigma_star=12, covert-key width K=2^19,
root13/other8 compression, and eps=eps_c=2^-208. Encryption accepts
nonempty identities of depth at most 162 and enforces 1<=g=|S|ceil(L/256)<=4.
The caps are Q<=2^32 ciphertext queries and N_K<=2^30 honest-key queries.
B bounds the registered population but does not itself cap oracle calls.

Channel mode/message privacy covers one family with the intended group,
first identity branch and payload length fixed before setup. Ordinary keys
are honestly generated and may be exposed, as may covert keys outside the
target group. Intended covert keys and sender coins remain private. Each
ciphertext uses fresh independent randomness. Additional-family key exposure
and adaptive changes of these fixed choices are outside this theorem.

Ordinary-message privacy has a separate scope. No honest secret key is
revealed. At every challenge, A1 requires pairwise distinct honest public-key
values at occupied selected leaves for its identity (raw vectors in the paper;
canonical prescribed encodings in this prototype). The restricted game
rejects inadmissible challenges in every hybrid. Setup and key generation
remain unconditioned, including accidental sample collisions. Canonically
mapping a value to its first oracle occurrence gives distinct selected pool
indices. A manager maintaining one subregistry per identity satisfies A1.
The low-level prototype accepts caller-chosen subregistries and does not
enforce that invariant or A1. Its duplicate-key regression demonstrates the
failure of the unrestricted independent-uniform endpoint and leakage of
per-slot message differences. It does not distinguish the common message.

For ordinary privacy, registration and challenge identities belong to one
prefix-free identity space; fixed-length encodings suffice. Registered-only prefix-freeness does
not ensure the required key-or-terminator final nodes for arbitrary challenge
identities. This is an ordinary-game precondition, not an API validation claim or a new
restriction on the root-only channel games.

Under these respective game conditions, the ideal-distribution bounds are
  Adv_mode <= Q*(a_n+a_(n-g)) + delta_mode,
  Adv_am <= 2Q*a_(n-g) + delta_mode,
  Adv_ordinary <= 2N_K*a_key + 2Q*(H-1)*a_G + 2Q*a_G_plus
                  + delta_ordinary,  H=163.
The bounded-key law is U{-32767,...,32768}. Gaussian challenges have
secret/error D_(R,12). The channel and non-leaf instances use 126 module
samples at the stated ranks. The rank 16 ordinary leaf embeds all honest
key responses and uses at most 126+N_K module samples. Each advantage is
evaluated at the complete reduction resources, including the larger key pool
and transcript simulation. Transferring ordinary privacy to anamorphic mode
also retains the intended-covert-key restriction of the mode game.

The enlarged leaf has secret-only leakage with weight nB=480. Its LLWE
ratio is 0.9900673140023465 and smoothing lower bound is 10.440018513374458<12.
The old generic weight 7nB fails at this sample count and is not used for the
leaf. Encryption parameters, root channel assumptions and statistical losses
are unchanged. See opus3-leaf-derivation.txt and opus3-leaf-checks.json.

The prior numerical sum remains below 2^-151 under A1 and the explicit
hypothesis that every relevant primitive advantage, including a_G_plus, is
at most 2^-192 at full reduction resources, with ideal independent randomness.
This is a sum of separate game and correctness bounds. Estimated attack costs
do not establish that primitive-advantage hypothesis. The implementation
requires an additional aggregate CSPRNG distinguishing term, not numerically
bounded here. Sampler approximation is accounted for separately.

Known-attack estimates use the scalarized module-LWE convention and chosen
Core-SVP models. The enlarged leaf has minimum completed finite costs of
316.236 classical and 286.995 quantum Core-SVP bits; coded-BKW fails
numerically and remains an evidence gap. The completed 126-sample rank 12 suite gives 219.0 classical
and 198.75 quantum bits. Enlarged-leaf coverage is recorded separately in
../../../research/history/experiments/implementation-results/rbe/results/opus3-leaf-estimator.json. Its execution status and omitted attacks
must accompany its numbers; the rank 12 result is not evidence for the larger
leaf sample count.

Registration inputs are prescribed honest key-generator outputs stored as
precomputed negative gadget decompositions. The API does not validate
arbitrary externally supplied predecomposed public keys. Its registry
operations assume prefix-free identities and do not implement the source
wrapper's complete merging lifecycle. The population capacity comes from
the source construction, not a 2^30-user deployment test. The code is a
variable-time research prototype. Its initial empty-registry state is repaired
and tested as recorded in initial-state-audit.txt.
